PRACTICAL, EVERYDAY GUIDANCE

Small habits that support your health

General guidance for adults. Your needs may differ based on age, pregnancy, disability, health conditions, medicines, and local recommendations.

Food and hydration

Choose a varied diet with vegetables, fruit, pulses and whole grains when available. Prefer safe drinking water. Needs vary; follow medical advice if you have a condition that affects food or fluids.

Move regularly

Build activity gradually in a way that feels safe for you. WHO’s adult guideline is 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity; some activity is better than none.

Sleep and mental wellbeing

Keep a regular sleep routine where possible and make time for rest and social connection. If distress is persistent or interferes with daily life, contact a qualified health professional.

Prevent infection and injury

Wash hands, follow food-safety practices, and ask a local health professional which vaccines and screening are recommended for you. Use seat belts and helmets; avoid tobacco and exposure to second-hand smoke.

Use medicines safely

Use medicines only as directed by a qualified professional or the official label. Check before combining medicines or supplements. Never share prescription medicines, and do not stop long-term treatment without advice.

Know when to get care

Arrange medical advice for new, persistent, worsening, or worrying symptoms. People with chronic conditions should follow their personalized care plan and routine check-ups.

Get emergency help now

Call your local emergency number for severe difficulty breathing, chest pressure or pain, fainting or unresponsiveness, severe bleeding, a seizure that will not stop, or sudden face drooping, arm weakness, or speech trouble. Do not wait for an online response.

This guide is general information, not personal medical advice or a diagnosis. Follow local public-health advice and a qualified clinician’s recommendations. Vaccine, screening, diet, and activity guidance can vary by person and country.
